Wear anything you can model.

Wings, halos, tails, capes and particles, worn on your player and set up entirely in an in-game editor. A client-side Fabric mod for Minecraft 26.3.

Download Rainium for Minecraft 26.3 See how it works

Free and open source. Rainium is in beta, so things may change between versions.

Your look, only on your screen

Rainium changes what you see and nothing else, so it works on any server and nothing needs installing there.

Wear any 3D model

Wings on your back, a halo behind your head, a tail, ears or anything else. Import your own OBJ, glTF or GLB files in the game.

Edit it live

Press K and change textures, colours, glow, size, position and angle while your player updates in real time. Everything saves as you go, with undo.

Natural movement

Cosmetics trail behind you as you run and turn, and sway gently when you stand still. Make each one as bouncy or as still as you like.

Particle emitters

Sparkles, embers, hearts, trails and auras, using Minecraft's particles or your own pictures.

Folders for multi-part models

Switch a whole model on or off, resize it or move it in one go.

Recolour with Hue

Shift a texture's colours around the colour wheel without editing the image.

Share your look

Export a folder as a zip, and a friend can add it from their editor.

Keeps itself up to date

New versions download in the background and install when you close the game. You can turn this off.

From model file to wearing it

You don't need to touch a config file. Everything below happens in the editor.

  1. Open the editor

    Press K in game. You can also use the /rainium command, or the Configure button in Mod Menu, and rebind the key in Controls.

  2. Try the example halo

    Rainium comes with a Gold halo that starts switched off. Click the square next to Gold halo in the layer list to show it.

  3. Add your own model

    Click Open rainium folder, drop in your .obj, .gltf or .glb and its texture, then pick it on the Add tab. Adjust size, angle and position on a live preview, and click Add as layer.

  4. Make it look right

    Select the layer and use the Edit tab:

    • Look: texture, style (solid, see-through, glow), hue and opacity.
    • Placement: body, head, arms or legs; size, position and angle.
    • Behaviour: a mirrored copy (model one wing, get two), swaying, hiding while gliding.
    • Shown on: which players and armor stands it appears on.

Hover any setting's name for a short explanation, and click any number to type an exact value. Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y undo and redo.

Particles, tuned by hand

A layer can give off particles instead of being a model. Start from a preset on the Add tab, then adjust it on the Edit tab.

Picture
One of Minecraft's particles (flame, sparkle, heart, note, smoke, petal and more) or any PNG of your own.
When and how many
Always, only while moving, only while standing still, or more the faster you move.
Where they appear
A point, a ball, a box or a ring around your body, head, arms or legs, at any size.
How they move
Direction, speed, spread, gravity, drag and spin, plus size over time and fading out.
Move with me
Particles either travel along with you or stay where they appeared, leaving a trail.

Models from any 3D program

OBJglTFGLB

Nearly every 3D program can export these, and Blender exports work with the default settings. A texture packed inside a .glb is picked up automatically, and a model with several parts becomes a folder.

To convert without opening the game, the repository has two converters in tools/:

  • obj-converter.html runs in your browser. Your model never leaves your computer.
  • mesh_convert.py is a command-line converter for all three formats, needing Python 3.8 or newer.

Sharing a look: open a folder in the layer list and click Export as zip. Your friend puts the zip in their config/rainium folder and adds it from Add › Shared sets.

Installing

  1. Install Fabric Loader for Minecraft 26.3, and Fabric API.
  2. Put the Rainium .jar in your .minecraft/mods folder.
  3. Launch the game and press K.

Mod Menu is optional. With it, Rainium's Configure button opens the editor too.

    Questions

    Can other players see my cosmetics?

    No. Rainium is client-side, so only you see what you set up. To share a look, send someone your files or an exported zip.

    Does it work on servers?

    Yes. It only runs on your side, so it works on any server and nothing needs to be installed there.

    Can I put my cosmetics on friends or on an armor stand?

    On your screen, yes. In Settings › Visibility, Show on can include everyone, other players, or a list of player names, and Armor stands shows them on armor stands with a name tag you choose. Nobody else sees this.

    Each layer and folder can also choose for itself under Shown on, for example wings just on you and a halo on an armor stand named "Display".

    How do updates work?

    Once per launch, Rainium checks GitHub for a newer version made for your Minecraft version. It downloads and checks it, then installs it when you close the game. Your layers and settings are kept.

    Only main releases (versions ending in .0, like 0.4.0) install automatically. Dev builds are early test versions: download them by hand, or switch on Include dev builds in Settings › Updates.

    Does Rainium collect any data?

    No. Its only network request is the optional update check, which means your game contacts GitHub, and GitHub can see your IP address. You can turn it off in Settings › Updates.

    When the editor shows a folder path, it replaces your computer's account name with your in-game name, so screenshots are safe to share. Change or hide it in Settings › Privacy.

    Where are my files?

    In .minecraft/config/rainium/. Open rainium folder in the editor takes you there. If a file you edited by hand has a problem, Rainium keeps showing your last working setup and explains the problem at the top of the editor.

    Can I translate it?

    Yes, please. Copy en_us.json to your language's code (for example de_de.json), translate the text on the right of each line, and send it in an issue. %s marks where a name or number goes.
